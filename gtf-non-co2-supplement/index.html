Contrails and non-CO₂: what's coming

Flying warms the planet in more ways than CO₂. Contrails and NOx add a lot of warming, but no one pays for it yet. This page shows what that warming is, why it comes as a range, and what the EU is doing about it.

The short version

01

It is a big part of the warming

Today, contrails and NOx cause about two thirds of the warming from flying. CO₂ causes about a third.

02

No one pays for it yet

EU ETS, UK ETS and CORSIA only price CO₂. The rest of the warming is free, for now.

03

The EU has started measuring

Since 1 January 2025, flights in the EU scheme have their non-CO₂ effects tracked. A decision on what comes next is due by the end of 2027.

Share of warming: Lee et al. (2021), effective radiative forcing for 2018.

Not every contrail warms

A contrail is a line of ice behind a plane. Most fade in seconds. The ones that matter form in very cold, humid air, spread into thin cloud and last for hours. Try it below.

Why it comes as a range

To put non-CO₂ warming in the same units as CO₂, studies use a multiplier: for every tonne of CO₂ a flight makes, how many tonnes of "CO₂-equivalent" warming it really causes. Three well-known estimates disagree.

CO₂ itself (1×)Extra from contrails and NOx
Why this is not the same as "two thirds": the two-thirds figure is the warming happening right now from all flights so far. A multiplier asks a different question: how much to add for a tonne of CO₂ from one flight today, over the next 100 years. Contrails warm hard but fade fast, so over 100 years they count for less. Different questions give different numbers.

This is a "what if", not a forecast. If the EU does price non-CO₂, it may use its own per-flight method instead of one multiplier for every flight. That could be much higher for some routes and close to zero for others.

What the EU is doing

  1. 1 January 2025
    Tracking starts

    Airlines in the EU scheme must monitor, report and verify non-CO₂ effects. The EU estimates them for each flight from flight data, the plane, the fuel and the weather.

  2. 17 July 2026
    A plan to reward contrail work

    The EU plans to set aside up to 3 million free allowances up to 2033, with no more than 420,000 a year, shared between airlines in the scheme (about €33.6m a year at today's price). Airlines would get them for planning flights around contrails and for better reporting. It is only a plan for now.

  3. By 31 December 2027
    The big decision

    The European Commission reports on what the tracking found and, if it sees fit, proposes a law to deal with non-CO₂ effects. That could mean bringing them into the EU carbon market.
