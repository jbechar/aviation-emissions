Which carbon rules cover a flight?

Point at a country to see which carbon rules it has. Or open the route checker. Pick two airports anywhere in the world and a plane, and it shows you which rules apply, draws the route, and guesses the carbon cost. For now it only covers the EU, UK and Swiss schemes and CORSIA. It is an early version, so don't treat it as the final word on the rules.

Add up a whole fleet

Add a row for each type of plane. Give it a rough route and how many flights it does a year. It adds up the carbon cost for the whole fleet, so you don't have to check one route at a time.

What this covers: the EU, UK and Swiss schemes, and CORSIA. The country shapes are rough, not exact borders. The rules text comes from the same places as the Climate rules page. Airports come from the OpenFlights list (about 6,000 airports with normal flights). The distance is the shortest line between the two airports. Real flights go further, because of air traffic control, busy skies and weather. I last checked carbon prices on 24 July 2026 (EU about €80 a tonne, UK about £72, CORSIA about $5). The UK price could move a lot while the UK and EU talk about joining their schemes, so check it before you use it. Fuel numbers are rough, based on the ICAO BFFM2 method and data from plane makers. Real costs change with how full the plane is, how high it flies, and the route. This is not advice. For what to file, or fines, ask a compliance advisor.
