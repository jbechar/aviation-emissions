    How it works and where the numbers come from

    Carbon cost = CO₂ under the EU scheme × EU price + CO₂ under the UK scheme × UK price + CO₂ under CORSIA × share to offset × credit price + required SAF × extra cost of SAF. Free allowances end in 2026 in both the EU and UK schemes. SAF: the EU requires 2% of fuel; the UK figure of 3.6% for 2026 sits on the straight line from 2% in 2025 to 10% in 2030. "Extra cost vs today" compares the scenario with today's prices and today's scope.

    Prices: EU €86.55 a tonne (6 Nov 2026). UK £60.17, the government's September 2026 average, about €72. CORSIA credits have no public price; US$20–25 is an estimate, about €20. The SAF figure of about €1,400 a tonne comes from Wizz Air paying €56.5m for roughly 27,000 tonnes, minus the price of normal jet fuel. Currency rates: European Central Bank, 6 Nov 2026.

    Wizz Air: results for the year to 31 March 2026. Wizz does not publish total CO₂, so it is estimated from 2.2 tonnes of fuel per block hour × 861,653 block hours × 3.16. easyJet: year to 30 September 2025; profit is headline profit before tax; CO₂ from its 2025 environmental factsheet. easyJet says 92% of its 2026 carbon is already bought at €64, which this page leaves out. Norwegian: 2025 results; CO₂ is its 2025 direct emissions.

    Profit is from the last reported year, which already paid carbon at lower prices with some free allowances. So the 2026 bill is a forward estimate, not a restatement of past results. Built from public data for illustration; not investment advice.
